# CK10.36 — todos' list, sk150's Presets and Device tabs, vaultview's folder picker, switch dialog and chooser at Always, read cold in both appearances

## What was reviewed

Two cold reads, each briefed with `reviews/misreads.md` read whole, given no checklist, and asked what a developer on macOS would complain about in each scrolling list; both were told that a standing bar drawn as a bare thumb with no track and a 10 px lane is already planned.

The first read twelve renders at 1x under Show scroll bars: Always, nothing scrolled: todos (650 × 600, 30 todos), sk150's Presets and Device tabs (720 × 560), vaultview's full-screen folder picker with 30 folders, the Open Vault switch dialog over the same folders, and the Choose a note chooser over 40 candidates (1100 × 800), each in light and dark. At 560 px sk150's rows fit their tabs, so no bar was drawn there; the second read took sk150's two tabs again at 720 × 320, where the rows overflow, in light and dark.

## The first reply, verbatim

Six of the twelve frames have nothing to complain about: both sk150 tabs and the folder picker, in both schemes. The two dialogs (the Open Vault folder browser and Choose a note) each have issues where the list meets its focus ring, and the todos window has one. Each issue looks the same in light and dark.

Pixels were sampled with a small pure-Python PNG decoder, since PIL is not installed. All coordinates are 1x, from the top-left of each PNG.

1. **The focus ring covers the edge of the list content (switchdialog and chooser, both schemes). Measured.** The ring is 4 px thick. In switchdialog it sits at x 288–291 and 808–811, and at y 309–312 and 501–504. Its inner 2 px fall inside the list. The selected row's fill starts at y 311 and x 290, under the ring: pixels at (500,311–312) and (290–291,320) are a blend of ring and selection fill, while the row's own fill starts only at y 313. The chooser has the same geometry: ring at y 202–205 and 658–661, selection fill starting at y 204. On macOS a focus ring is drawn outside the scroll view's frame and never over its rows, so a Mac developer would call this the ring eating 2 px of the first row and of the selection.

2. **The thumb has no inset from the ends of its lane or from the box (switchdialog and chooser). Measured.** In switchdialog the thumb spans x 802–807 and y 313–362. Its top is the first content row (y 313), and its right edge touches the ring's inner edge at x 808 with no gap. The chooser is the same: thumb at y 206–348, starting on the first content row, and x 802–807 against the ring at 808. A macOS scroller keeps a few pixels between the thumb and the ends of its track, so here it looks jammed into the corner. The picker's thumb also starts at the list's top row (y 90, the first pixel row that differs from the background), but nothing borders it there, so it reads as less wrong (visual judgement).

3. **The selection highlight stops short of the box and leaves a white strip by the thumb (switchdialog and chooser). Measured.** The selected row's fill ends at x 799. Columns 800–807 are the list background (#ffffff light, #1e1e1e dark), and the thumb sits inside that strip, so there is a notch between the highlight and the ring. With overlay scroll bars, macOS runs the selection under the thumb; with legacy bars, a track with its own fill occupies that strip. Whether the planned track change fixes this depends on the track painting columns 800–807 (the bare thumb itself is excluded as already planned).

4. **Todos: the floating + button covers the last visible row's delete button, and the list leaves no room at its end for it.** Measured part: the + button spans x 594–633 and y 544–583, the last row's delete X is drawn at x about 584–608 and y about 576–600, and the thumb lane is at x 642–647. Visual read: when scrolled to the end, the last todo's X will sit under the + button unless the list adds bottom padding of about the button's height. A Mac developer would object to a button that floats over list content.

5. **sk150 Presets and Device, both schemes: nothing to complain about. Measured.** No pixels differ from the background in x 693–719 below the tabs, so no bar is drawn. Both grids fit their windows (10 rows on Presets; the Device grid ends well above the footer line). macOS likewise shows no scroller for a list that fits.

6. **vaultview picker, both schemes: nothing to complain about. Thumb measured, the rest visual reads.** The thumb spans x 878–883 and y 90–654. The list ink ends at y 727, where row 26 is cut off. The thumb's length, 565 of 637 px, matches about 26.5 visible rows out of 30. Rows cut off at the bottom edge are normal for a scrolling list. The blank stand-in button below is excluded as you said.

Thumb lengths check out in every frame. Switchdialog: 50 px of 188 for about 7.8 of 30 rows. Chooser: 143 px of 452 for about 12.6 of 40 rows. Todos: 164 px of 566, which would mean about 30 rows; I couldn't confirm that count from the frame. I made no colour claims beyond the sampled values quoted above.

## The second reply, verbatim

I found two problems with how the scrolling lists end at the bottom, and one possible problem with the Presets column headings. The headless parts and the planned bare-thumb bar are left out. Pixels were sampled with a pure-Python PNG decoder. Light and dark frames have the same geometry in every row I measured.

1. **Device tab, both frames: the next row's field tops show through, touching the footer.** The grid is cut off at y=283. Rows y=281–282 show 2 px of the next row's text-field top borders, at x=84–187 and x=420–523. The footer text starts at y=285, so its ascenders are 2–3 px below those slivers. There is no gap or separator between the cut edge and the footer. A developer would see two stray grey hairlines over the footer, or a broken underline, rather than content scrolling under an edge. The footer reads as if it were part of the scrolling grid. *Measured:* the y=283 cut, the sliver rows, their x-ranges and the footer top at 285. *Visual read:* how it comes across.

2. **Presets tab, both frames: the list stops 16 px above the window bottom, with an empty band below it.** Row content (the Recall buttons, the M4 text) ends hard at y=303/304. Rows y=304–319 are plain background: (255,255,255) in light and (30,30,30) in dark. So row M4 is cut through its glyphs at a line inside the window, not at the window or view edge. Nothing marks the edge and there is no fade. On macOS a scroll view runs to its frame edge, so a developer would ask why content vanishes into blank space above the window bottom. *Measured:* the y=304 cut and the 16 px empty band. *Visual read:* that M4's glyphs are cut through.

3. **Presets tab, both frames (visual inference, not proven from a still): the column headings scroll away with the rows.** The bar's thumb starts at y=98, just under the tab divider at y=95. That divider spans x=16–703. So the scrolling region starts above the "Memory slots" heading (top at y=114) and includes the "M V A LVP OVP …" heading row at about y=174. After a short scroll the columns would lose their labels. A macOS developer expects a table's column headings to stay pinned, as NSTableView's do. *Measured:* where the thumb starts and where the divider and heading sit. *Inferred:* that the heading row scrolls.

Nothing else in the lists is worth a complaint:
- The bar doesn't collide with content. The Recall buttons end at x=689 and the thumb sits at x=696–701, 6 px away. That was measured.
- The thumb sits 3 px under the divider, which is fine.
- I sampled the thumb's colour and it is neutral grey: (109,109,109) in light and (159,159,159) in dark. I make no other hue claims.

The top of both lists has nothing to complain about.
