# CK10.45 — a code block's sideways bar in vaultview's note, read cold in both appearances

Eight renders of vaultview's note column at 534 × 700, 1x, a note holding a fenced code block whose first line is far wider than the column: light and dark at the platform setting Always and at When scrolling, each at rest and just after a 200 px sideways wheel scroll over the block. The renders came from a temporary test calling `layoutNotePage` with the setting on the tokens; the test was removed after the capture. The reviewer was given the Scroll area and Scrollbar entries verbatim, told that the missing track fill and the unmeasured lane, track and thumb at Always are filed already (finding 1137), and briefed with the full text of `reviews/misreads.md` pasted into the prompt.

## The reply, verbatim

I read the recorded misreads file in full, and nothing below repeats it or the already-filed missing track fill and unmeasured lane, track and thumb.

All coordinates are in px at 1x, sampled with PIL. The block is the same in all eight frames:
- **Border:** 1 px, at x=24 and x=509. The top edge is at y=165. The bottom edge is at y=236 at Always and y=226 at When scrolling.
- **Inside:** x=25..508.
- **Fill:** light (239,241,245), border (216,217,221). Dark (30,30,46), border (52,52,66).
- **Code text:** x≈36..497 and y≈180..211.
- **Padding:** about 11–12 px at the sides and about 14 px at the top and bottom (y 166→180 and 211→225).

**Answers to the four questions**

1. **Where the bar is drawn.** In both settings it is at the block's bottom edge, inside the block, and not inset by the padding.
   - At Always, the thumb fills y=228..233 (6 px tall). That leaves 2 px above it (226–227) and 2 px below it (234–235) before the border at y=236.
   - At When scrolling, the thumb fills y=218..223, 2 px above the border at y=226.
   - At rest the thumb starts at x=27, 2 px from the block's inside leading edge (x=25). After the 200 px scroll it spans x=99..263.
   - This passes the entries.

2. **Lane or no lane.** Both settings pass the entries.
   - **Always:** the block is 10 px taller than at When scrolling (bottom border at 236 against 226). The extra rows, y=226..235, are a lane for the bar. The code and its bottom padding end at y=225, the same row as at When scrolling.
   - **When scrolling:** there is no lane. The padding runs to y=225 and the thumb (218..223) lies over it.
   - **When scrolling, at rest:** no thumb pixels at all; rows 212–223 are pure fill. That matches "at rest there is no thumb".

3. **Bar over the padding at Always, or inset by it.** Neither, which passes the entries. At Always the thumb sits only in the lane rows (y≥226) and never on the padding rows. It starts at x=27, not at the 12 px padding inset (x≈36).

**Findings**

1. **The block's side padding does not scroll with the code. It stays put and the code is clipped at its inner edge, so code never reaches the block's edge.** This is the main finding.
   - In the scrolled frames, no code pixel lies in x=25..35 or x=498..508. On every text row the leftmost pixel is at x≥36 and the rightmost at x≤497.
   - The Scrollbar entry says the region that scrolls includes its padding. On macOS, a text view's inset scrolls with the text, so scrolled text runs to the edge of the visible area.
   - The thumb arithmetic agrees: the scrolling viewport is the padded inner box, not the block.
     - A 200 px scroll moves the thumb 72 px (27→99), and the thumb is 165 px wide.
     - Visible width = 165 × 200 / 72 ≈ 458 px. That matches the padded box (36..496, about 460 px), not the block's inside (484 px).
   - So the bar is drawn across the whole block, but what actually scrolls is the padded box inside it.

2. **The code fades out at the clipped edges.**
   - **At rest, trailing edge:** glyphs at x≈485..497 fade toward the fill. In light, (233,235,240) at x=496 against about (76,79,105) for normal text.
   - **Scrolled, leading edge too:** light glyphs at x=39..44 are (188..229) against about 76 for normal text. Dark glyphs near x=497 are (41,42,57).
   - AppKit scroll views and the platform's code views (Xcode, Notes, Safari reading views) clip text hard. They do not fade it.
   - Combined with finding 1, a macOS developer sees text fading out 12 px short of the block's border on both sides. That reads as a web-page effect, not a native scroll view.

3. **The thumb is the same at both settings and looks opaque.** I give this lower weight because it partly overlaps the filed "thumb not measured".
   - The thumb is the same 6 px capsule with the same 2 px insets at Always and at When scrolling.
   - Its colour is the same too: light (104,105,105), dark (164,164,164).
   - Both colours are neutral grey even though the fill is tinted blue. A semi-transparent black or white thumb would pick up the blue. For example, white at about 50% over (30,30,46) would give about (164,164,171). These look like solid greys.
   - On the platform, the standing scroller at Always and the overlay scroller at When scrolling look different from each other. Here they look identical.

4. **Things that are fine:**
   - The block is 10 px shorter at When scrolling than at Always, so the paragraph below sits 10 px higher. That is the lane at work, as the entries require, not a defect.
   - Top and bottom padding are equal, at 14 px each.
   - The thumb does not touch the rounded corners, which take up only rows 166–167 and 234–235 at x=25 and x=508.
   - Light and dark behave the same way geometrically.

## Disposition

Finding 1 is filed as finding 1167 and finding 2 as finding 1168; both are beside this task's subject. Finding 3 splits in two. That the standing and the fading thumb look alike, and how the standing thumb should look, is finding 1137.
